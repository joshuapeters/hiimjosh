I've been re-reading Lord of the Rings. It's my first time as an adult that I've read Tolkien's work, the first time being in late middle/early high school.

What always struck me about the books that the movies couldn't quite capture was that the threat Mordor imposes on the world isn't as literal as the Ringwraiths, or the arms of orcs and uruk-hai. The movie paints the threat in these shades because they show up well on the big screen. The book, however, paints the dangers and encroaching power of Mordor as a shadow looming over Middle-Earth. It's tendrils slowly reaching into and corrupting the hearts of the most fallible races of the realm.

I'm beginning to realize that so much of our world lives in these terms. There is rarely an obvious, huge, objective evil that we can point to and say that this is going to ruin our lives. Rather, it's how the evil corrupts us, that slowly destroys us. The LLM marketting machine is trying to convince everyone that AI is a threat to their lives; a huge looming and obvious threat. This is exactly what the film adaptations of Lord of the Rings does: it points at the orc armies and Uruk-Hai hives, demanding you understand how dangerous these are. How _impressive_ this is. We should fear it and expect it to murder us all, while we continue to buy our tokens and AI LARPers show off their Jarvis Loops :tm: on TikTok. This is not the Uruk-Hai army clanging the shields at our gates, this is Sauruman explaining that the Uruk-Hai are extremely dangerous while also selling them to us to be our gardners. We are not in danger; we are corrupted.

----

My company laid off 37 people last week, which is roughly 20% of the company. When guaging the opinions of those around me, I was shocked to hear that many weren't surprised by this. They had either been part of layoffs before, or saw the writing on the wall in our monthly financial reports. I, on the other hand, was shocked and caught off guard.

Things never felt great, but never felt dire. It felt more like a storm we needed to weather and a moment where the macroeconomics were working against us. However, we've got money in the bank, a good team, and time. Then, on Wednesday morning, the all-hands meeting was scheduled and slack profiles went dark.

A monetary shortfall was cited, and that we needed to make it up with the money gained back by reducing headcount. Several departments were impacted all across the org.

The ones least impacted appear to be the ones building our AI automation platform, which currently costs us ~40k a month to run, and has a few million dollars in headcount. The teams cited as being the most important in driving revenue had their teams downsized and restructured. I was part of the latter. Those laid off will find opportunities and continue on, our time together becoming a line in their resume and a talking point in their interviews. Meanwhile, the thing that lead to them being laid of is still invested in and staffed, trudging along with the hope of seeing ROI someday. We are not in danger; we are corrupted.
